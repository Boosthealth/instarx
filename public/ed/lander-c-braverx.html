The ingredient most ED medication is missing

Apomorphine is a dopamine agonist that works on the central nervous system rather than the vascular system. It acts on the part of the brain that initiates the sexual response, which is a different circuit from the one that executes it.

Every other medication in the category addresses blood flow. Apomorphine addresses whether the signal that starts the whole thing ever arrives. It is the reason a stronger blue pill never fixed it for some men, and it is not a stronger anything.

FAQs

How is this different from generic medication?
A generic gives you one PDE5 inhibitor. This gives you three of them layered, plus a fourth active that is not a PDE5 inhibitor at all and works on arousal instead of blood flow. If a generic already works for you, a combination product is a more expensive way to get the same result.
What is apomorphine?
A centrally-acting dopamine agonist, unrelated to Viagra and not a stimulant. It was studied for erectile dysfunction in the early 2000s and then shelved because it came off patent rather than because it failed. It is FDA-approved in the United States as a medicine for a different indication.
Do I need a prescription?
Yes. A US-licensed physician reviews your intake and your full medication list before anything is prescribed or shipped. If it is not appropriate for you, it is not prescribed and you are not charged.
Is it FDA-approved?
The individual active ingredients are FDA-approved medicines. The compounded combination is not approved as a combination product, and the FDA does not verify the safety or effectiveness of compounded drugs.
Who should not take it?
Anyone taking nitrates for chest pain. Men with severe uncontrolled hypertension, recent stroke or unstable cardiovascular disease. Men on certain Parkinson's medications, because apomorphine interacts with that class. Disclose your full medication list on the intake.
