#1

It wakes the pathway your prescription was never built to reach

Sildenafil, tadalafil and vardenafil are all PDE5 inhibitors. So are the chewables, the mints and the generic tablets. They open blood flow, which is genuinely most of the problem for most men, and if that is your gap they work.

But they have no mechanism of action on arousal at all. Not a weak one. None. So if the physical side already works and you still feel absent during sex, a stronger PDE5 inhibitor is a larger dose of the wrong answer.

Frequently asked questions

I already take something that works. Why would I switch?
If it works and you feel present during sex, you probably should not. This page is for the specific man whose mechanics are fine and who still feels absent, because that is the pathway a PDE5 inhibitor does not touch. The intake asks about this directly and a physician sees your answer.
Other brands advertise the same four ingredients. Why this one?
Because the ingredient list stopped being the difference, and we would rather say so than pretend. What is left to compare is format, how fast the decision happens, whether the milligrams are published, who reviews your intake and what it costs to find out. We currently lose on published doses and we have said so twice on this page.
Is it FDA-approved?
The individual active ingredients are FDA-approved medicines. The compounded combination is not approved as a combination product, and the FDA does not verify the safety or effectiveness of compounded drugs. Apomorphine is FDA-approved in the United States for a different indication and is used here as part of a compounded formulation.
Who cannot take this?
Anyone taking nitrates for chest pain. Men with severe uncontrolled hypertension, recent stroke or unstable cardiovascular disease. Men on certain Parkinson's medications, because apomorphine is a dopamine agonist and interacts with that class. Disclose your full medication list on the intake, because the review only protects you if it is accurate.
What are the side effects?
Headache, flushing and nasal congestion are documented across the PDE5 class and are the most commonly reported. Apomorphine can cause nausea, more commonly at higher doses. Seek immediate medical attention for an erection lasting more than four hours.
I am on a GLP-1. Where does that leave me?
Put it on the intake so the physician sees it. Low libido is commonly reported by men on these medications and the mechanism is not established. If you lost the weight and the drive did not come back with it, that is an arousal-side question rather than a blood-flow one, but whether this is the right answer for you is clinical rather than promotional.
What happens if the doctor says no?
You are not charged, and you have learned something useful for free. A decline usually means something in your history needs a different approach, which is worth knowing either way.
