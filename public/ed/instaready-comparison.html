How to read your own row

Three questions decide this, and none of them is about how many molecules are in the bottle.

One: does the physical response arrive at all? If you take a standard pill and nothing happens, your gap is vascular, and the sensible direction is a stronger or faster PDE5 approach, or if oral has genuinely failed, the injectable. Adding an arousal-side molecule will not fix a plumbing problem.

Two: does it work, but you feel absent during it? Then your gap is on the arousal side and no amount of additional PDE5 inhibitor reaches it. That points to one of the combination formats carrying a centrally-acting molecule, which is rows four and five.

Three: is the medication fine and the scheduling the thing you hate? Then the answer is the daily low dose, and it is the cheapest useful answer on this page. Adding actives does not solve a timing problem. Changing the schedule does.

One thing to check on any page in this category, including this one. Several brands describe a compounded multi-ingredient formula as FDA-approved. That is not accurate for any of them. The individual active ingredients are FDA-approved; the compounded combination is not approved as a combination product, and the FDA does not verify the safety or effectiveness of compounded drugs.

If a page gets that wrong, treat everything else it tells you with the same weight.

Common questions

Is more actives always better?
No. More actives means more mechanisms covered, which helps only if your gap sits on a pathway the simpler product does not reach. If one generic active already works for you, a four-ingredient formula is a more expensive way to get the same result with more side effect surface.
Which of these is strongest?
The injectable, clearly and by a distance. That is why it stays on this page despite being the option men most want to avoid. If oral medication has genuinely failed you, that is the conversation to have with a clinician, and no liquid or tablet on this page replaces it.
Why does the wait matter so much?
Because the wait is what forces the planning, and the planning is what most couples actually describe as the problem. An hour means somebody decides in the afternoon that there is going to be an evening. Ten or fifteen minutes means nobody has to decide anything in advance. That is a different life, not a different drug.
Who should not take any of these?
Anyone taking nitrates for chest pain should avoid every oral option here. Men with severe uncontrolled hypertension, recent stroke or unstable cardiovascular disease should not take any product in this comparison. Apomorphine specifically interacts with certain Parkinson's medications and can cause nausea. This is what a prescriber review is for.
I am on a GLP-1. Where does that put me?
Probably in row four or five, and definitely on a form where a physician can see it. Low libido is commonly reported by men on GLP-1 medications and the mechanism is not established. If you lost the weight and the drive did not return with it, that is an arousal-side question rather than a blood-flow one.
